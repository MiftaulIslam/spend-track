The problem with expense trackers

A transfer is not an expense. Most apps get this wrong.

Move $500 from checking to savings and a naive tracker counts it as spending. Pay your credit card and it double-counts every charge. SpendTrack models money movement properly: transfers net to zero, card payments settle a liability, refunds reverse the original expense against the original category and period.

What you get

One ledger for everything you own and owe.

Every account type

Cash, checking, savings, credit and debit cards, prepaid, digital wallets, brokerage, retirement, loans — each with the behaviour it actually has.

Recurring that behaves

Daily to yearly, fixed or variable, with pause, skip and weekend handling. Bills are estimated from history until you confirm.

Budgets with pace

A budget without a pace marker is a number. Ours shows whether you're ahead or behind for where you are in the period.

Real multi-currency

Accounts hold their own currency. Conversions use the rate on the transaction date, not today's.

Receipts that file themselves

Photograph it. Claude reads merchant, total, tax and line items, then matches it to the charge that already posted.

An assistant with limits

It can search, summarise and propose. Anything that changes your data stops and asks. It cannot reach the database directly.

Security posture

Built like something that holds your money data.

  • Every server operation re-checks ownership. Client-supplied IDs are never trusted.
  • Financial writes run inside database transactions — a transfer either moves both sides or neither.
  • The AI never receives credentials or database access. It calls a fixed set of audited tools under your permissions.
  • Uploads are MIME-sniffed, size-limited and stripped of metadata.
  • Every financial change is recorded with actor, timestamp and before/after values.
